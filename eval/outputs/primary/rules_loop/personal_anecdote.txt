The first time I cooked for someone I wanted to impress: I picked a recipe I'd never made before. Brilliant decision.

It was a pasta dish that looked simple in the video: brown some sausage, soften onions, deglaze with wine, finish with cream. What the video did not mention was that deglazing a hot pan makes a tremendous amount of smoke, or that "soften" and "burn" are separated by about forty-five seconds of distraction.

She arrived to find every window in my apartment open, me fanning a smoke detector with a dish towel, and a pan of onions that were, let's say, deeply caramelized.

I considered ordering pizza. Instead I started over from scratch, calmer, working more slowly. The second attempt was genuinely good. Not impressive, but good.

We talked through the whole meal. At some point she said the dinner was fine, and I think she meant the company more than the food. That was probably right.
