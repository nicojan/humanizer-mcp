I made pasta. Specifically, I made pasta because it was the one thing I'd cooked enough times to feel confident about, and confidence seemed like the only advantage I had going into that evening.

He arrived at seven. I had the sauce going, the water almost boiling, and a playlist I'd spent longer curating than I'd spent practicing the actual recipe. The apartment smelled right. I felt, briefly, like I had this.

Then I overcooked the pasta by about four minutes while I was distracted trying to look calm. It went from al dente to something closer to polenta. I drained it anyway and tossed it in the sauce like the texture had been intentional.

He didn't say anything. He had two servings.

I've since learned that the meal matters less than I thought it did. What people remember is whether you were present, whether the table felt good, whether someone was genuinely glad they showed up. He was. I was.

We've eaten a lot of pasta since then, and I've gotten considerably better at timing it.
