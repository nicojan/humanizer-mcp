I decided to make pasta from scratch. I'd watched my mother do it a hundred times, so naturally I assumed I could do it once without asking her how.

The dough came together fine. I felt confident enough to be smug about it. Then I tried to roll it thin and discovered that pasta dough has opinions. I rolled, it sprang back. I pressed harder, it tore. I patched the tears. By the time I'd cut the noodles they were more rectangular than long, and about as uniform as a pile of napkins.

She arrived while I was still boiling them, the kitchen smelling strongly of garlic and whatever I'd done to the parmesan. I served it with a lot of sauce to hide the shapes.

She said it was good. She had seconds. I don't know if she meant it, but she ate everything, and I realized that cooking for someone you care about is more about the trying than the result.
