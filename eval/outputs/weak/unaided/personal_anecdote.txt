I was nervous the moment she accepted my dinner invitation. I'd checked three recipes before settling on a pasta dish—something I'd made once before, which felt safe. I spent the afternoon chopping vegetables with careful, deliberate strokes, watching YouTube clips on sauce consistency. When she arrived, I was already sweating in the kitchen. The garlic burned slightly. The pasta stuck together. But halfway through the meal, she smiled at something I'd said and reached for more. Later, while I was clearing plates, she mentioned the salad was perfectly dressed. It wasn't perfect—nothing about that dinner was. But what mattered wasn't the flawless execution. She came back for seconds. She laughed. We talked long after the dishes were washed. That taught me something I couldn't have learned from a recipe: cooking for someone you care about isn't about achieving perfection. It's about showing up, trying sincerely, and letting the effort speak for itself.
