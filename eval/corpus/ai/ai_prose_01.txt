When I was small, the line between work and play seemed obvious. Play was anything I'd have done on my own. Work was the stuff grown-ups invented to ruin a perfectly good afternoon: rake the leaves, set the table, finish the math sheet before the cartoons. Nobody had to teach me the difference. I felt it in my gut, the way you feel the difference between a hill you're running down and one you're trudging up.

So I grew up assuming a job was just chores with a paycheck stapled to them. Something done because somebody made you, not because you wanted to. It took me an embarrassingly long time to notice the cracks in that idea, to meet adults who chased their work the way I once chased fireflies. I'm still unlearning it. Some mornings the old kid-logic creeps back in, and the alarm clock sounds exactly like my mother calling me inside.
