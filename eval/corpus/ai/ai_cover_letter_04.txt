I believe a classroom works best when children feel safe enough to be wrong out loud. In my four years teaching third and fourth grade, I have built my practice around that idea, designing lessons that invite kids to guess, test, and revise rather than wait nervously for the one right answer. When a student feels free to take a risk, the learning takes care of itself.

In practice, that means a lot of hands-on work and a lot of talking through problems together. I use small reading groups so I can meet each child where they actually are, and I lean on math manipulatives so that abstract ideas have something concrete to stand on. I also keep families in the loop, because the children who thrive are usually the ones whose parents and teacher are pulling in the same direction. I would be honored to bring that approach to your school.
