For four years I worked as a case aide at a community center serving children who had been referred to us by schools, social workers, and the courts. Many of them arrived guarded, having learned that adults don't always stick around, and a good part of my job was simply proving otherwise week after week. I ran our after-school homework club, coordinated transportation for kids whose families couldn't manage the pickup, and sat in on the case reviews where our staff decided how best to support each child.

That experience taught me that coordinating a program for vulnerable children is as much about logistics as it is about heart. Schedules slip, funding shifts, and a single missed handoff can mean a child falls through the cracks. I learned to keep the moving pieces organized without ever losing sight of the kids at the center of them, and I would bring that same steadiness to the coordinator role you've posted.
