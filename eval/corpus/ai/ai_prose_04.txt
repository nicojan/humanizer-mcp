I found a shoebox of my old drawings last month, tucked behind the winter coats. Hundreds of them. Spaceships, mostly, and maps of countries that never existed, drawn on the backs of my dad's office printouts. I must have been nine or ten. I'd forgotten I ever did this, and there it was in front of me, proof that I once spent whole weekends inventing worlds and didn't think it strange at all.

Somewhere around thirteen I just stopped. Not a dramatic quitting, no decision I can point to. The pencils went into a drawer and the drawer got buried under other things, the way everything does at that age. I think I decided drawing was something little kids did, and I had bigger ideas about who I was becoming.

What gets me is that nobody made me stop. I gave it up all on my own, for reasons I can't even reconstruct now. So last week I bought a sketchbook. The first pages are terrible. I don't care even a little.
