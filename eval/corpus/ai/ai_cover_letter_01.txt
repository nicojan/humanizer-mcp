Over the past five years I have taught first-year composition, advanced argumentation, and a sophomore seminar in rhetorical theory, designing each syllabus around the conviction that students write best when they have something real at stake in the page. My composition sections move students from personal narrative toward research-driven argument over the term, and I have found that the bridge between those modes is where the most durable learning happens. I treat revision not as cleanup but as rethinking, and I structure my courses so that no major essay is submitted without at least one full round of substantive rewriting.

Alongside the classroom, I spent three years staffing our university writing center, where I worked one-on-one with students from every discipline. Those tutoring hours taught me to diagnose a draft quickly and to ask the question that helps a writer find their own way forward.
